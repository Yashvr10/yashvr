Herodotus was told that the Great Pyramid was built by gangs of 100,000 men. Ever since, the story of a vast slave army has been repeated, even in modern news reports. But Herodotus never called them slaves, and the builders left their own evidence: a town with bakeries and royal grain silos, tombs with medical care, gang names painted on the stones, and an inspector's logbook more than 4,500 years old.

This film counts the workforce from that evidence. The answer: thousands, tens of thousands at the peak. Organised, fed and buried with honour.
