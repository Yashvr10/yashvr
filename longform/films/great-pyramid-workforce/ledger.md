## Spread of estimates (what the film argues from)
Ancient: 100,000 (L01), 360,000 (L04). Modern: ~5,000 core builders (L12), 10,000 (L22), 20,000 (L15), 36,000 (L14), 40,000 peak (L16).
COMPUTED: the highest modern figure (40,000) is 2.5x smaller than Herodotus (100,000 / 40,000 = 2.5) and 9x smaller than Diodorus (360,000 / 40,000 = 9).

## Stage 3: hostile fact-check and resolution (20 Oct 2026)
Run by a fresh helper agent given only factcheck-packet.md, with web access. Result: 20 CONFIRMED, 1 WRONG (L25 attribution), 3 confirmed with reservations (L09, L16, L23), 1 CANNOT CHECK (L17); all COMPUTED rows re-derived correctly.
Resolutions (decided by Claude under the owner's delegation; the owner was away):
- L01: Herodotus never says "slaves"; he says Kheops "compelled all the Egyptians to work for him" (Perseus 2.124.1, opened). The film uses this.
- L05: say "about 147 m" if used (Petrie 1883: 146.7 m +/- 0.2). Not used in the script.
- L09: unfootnoted in Wikipedia. NOT USED.
- L11: revised to a range. Sources give 12-hour (Archaeology 2001) and sunrise-to-sunset 14-hour days (Hawass, NOVA). COMPUTED: 600/315.07 = 1.90 min; 840/315.07 = 2.67 min, so "one block every two to three minutes". The 10-hour figure was my assumption.
- L13: keep Lehner's "conscripts" context. The film says "not slaves" is not the same as free.
- L15: wording "5,000 full-time specialists and administrators plus 15,000 laborers" (Rose 2001, summarising Hawass in a PBS film).
- L16: always say "contractor" and "on a ten-year schedule".
- L17: CANNOT CHECK. NOT USED.
- L20: Hawass was Egypt's archaeology chief at the time (AP 2010).
- L21: attribute to Hawass; no method given.
- L22: shown only as part of the spread ("what we don't know").
- L24: "about 40" is Tallet's estimate, not a number in the diary.
- L25: CORRECTED. Boat load = Tallet's estimate, "about 30 blocks at 2.5 tons each", cited by Lehner 2020 (opened, PDF text line 2932-2934). "Two or three trips every ten days" traces to a 2018 TV documentary: UNVERIFIED, NOT USED.
- Ratios: Herodotus's 100,000 is 2.5 times, and Diodorus's 360,000 is 9 times, the highest modern peak estimate (40,000). They measure different things (a gang, a total, a peak), so the film says "well under" and "nine times", not exact equivalence.
