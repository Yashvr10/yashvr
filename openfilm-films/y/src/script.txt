Are you on the right path?

Continuing to back yourself even when it feels like the rest of the world is against you is an extremely valuable and potentially dangerous skill to learn.
Great men have been forged this way, but so have losers. Relentlessly beating your head against the wall can lead to either the wall crumbling, or your head splitting open.
But what if you could just walk around the wall?
What if you’re making life unnecessarily difficult for yourself?
What if you’re actually on the wrong path beating your head against the wrong wall?
Imagine almost falling apart, almost dying, only to realise you’ve chosen the wrong wall. All that effort, all that time, all that risk, for what?
Something you don’t even want?

It’s a gamble. We win some, we lose some. But it’s up to us to stack the odds in our favour. To do the work to get to the point where we genuinely believe this is what we want.
And I’m not necessarily talking about hypnotising yourself into wanting it. Rather about entering the mental and emotional state of already having it and diligently observing your reaction.

What’s the feeling like? How long does it last? What’s your thought process like? When the initial high wears off, what’s it like now? Do you still want it? Do you want everything that comes with it? Having sacrificed and paid the price to get the thing, was it worth it? If you could go back, would you do it all over again?

The art here lies in trying to be objectively subjective in your approach. In trying to genuinely figure out what it’d be like, what you’d think, and what you want.
It’s easy to be led astray here, to get seduced by the glorious promise of the upside and ignore everything else. And it makes sense, because usually that wonderful upside can feel so desirable. Especially when we’ve romanticised and dreamed about it.
It’s so fascinating because a lot of the time this romanticisation is the product of our environment, not us. We’ve accepted it, but haven’t been in charge of it, and sometimes we’ll find that what we’re pursuing is actually that important and magical to us, but a lot of the time it isn’t.

Most people realise this far too late: after years of effort, after huge amounts of money spent, after lost professional and personal opportunities, after lost mental peace.
But you don’t have to be most people. That’s the beauty of it. You don’t need to stumble your way through life and make lots of predictable mistakes and miscalculations. You can literally just learn from and observe other people’s mistakes and successes instead.

And what you learn won’t always perfectly align with the kinda life you want, which is why it’s so important to invest in figuring out what you want and in getting a pretty good idea of what it’ll be like when you actually get there and have the thing.
Embracing this successful perspective is incredibly valuable because you’ll then be able to get better answers when you ask yourself what the successful version of you, who’s already achieved what you’re currently trying to achieve, would do in your situation.

This can be so useful because a lot of the time, you already know what to do, or at least when you think about it from this successful state, your intuition gives you a suggestion about what you should do.
Following this intuition isn’t always the easiest thing in the world to do, but as you listen to and follow it over time, it’ll become stronger and easier to notice. It’s pretty much like developing a sixth sense. Like you get access to more information than you’re able to consciously perceive in front of you.
It’s so valuable, yet also easy to overthink and mistake for something else. The art lies in not really trying to force it, but in opening yourself up and really listening. It can feel like a whisper sometimes, like it’s shouting from underwater or behind a wall.

And although I’m no master of listening to and using it yet, I’ve found it to be really helpful. The quality of my life and operation in this world has definitely improved a lot since I started using it more.
I can’t always see the bigger picture at first, but as I move with faith and try to stay aligned with what the successful version of me would do, the path that unfolds tends to move me closer to the life I want.

There are many peaks and valleys along the way, trials and tribulations, sunny and stormy days, but I’m always striving to make progress; sometimes I make a lot and sometimes a little. But progress is progress.
This progress-making is such a beautiful thing, actually taking concrete steps every day towards what you want most in this world. It’s something a lot of people don’t get to experience much of.
But like we’ve already established, you don’t need to be most people. You can literally just decide you’ll live a super fulfilling life. Go out and explore what this means to you, and then make progress on it daily.

There’s no need to overcomplicate it. Don’t put a fulfilling and amazing life on a pedestal. Get onto the same level as it and then stay here. Pursue and actually get it.
Challenge yourself. Be present. Stay aligned. Enjoy life on multiple levels, both on a deeper and on a more surface level.

This combination will actually make your life so much better when you let it.
So let it.
