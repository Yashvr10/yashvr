# Disenchantment of the World
> "The fate of our times is characterized by rationalization and intellectualization and, above all, by the disenchantment of the world." Max Weber, Science as a Vocation.
First things first. What you have to realize is that larping, or 'acting as if', in itself isn't some new fancy trend from TikTok. It is, in fact, an echo of the way humans were originally meant to live.
For most of human evolution, everything in life was woven out of gods, myths and tales.
Each member of society lived inside his own sacred story. A myth that told him who he was, and what his life was meant for.
Back then, a stonemason laying bricks wasn't just stacking stones for a paycheck. He was building the 'house of God', the place where heaven touched the earth. So he treated his work as a sacred duty.
Nowadays it has all been replaced with a gray machine. Everything running on intellect and calculation. The very same man lays bricks for an office building, gets paid by the hour, and comes home wondering why he is depressed.
Both are playing a certain role, with the difference that the modern one has no myth to live by, therefore no reason to give his work everything he has.
Max Weber called it 'the disenchantment of the world': the gradual draining of magic out of human life.
But it isn't just some sobby story of the human condition. It is, in fact, a perfect mirror of the process that happened to every one of us along the way.
Back then, when we were kids, we were knights, explorers of space, emperors of the backyard.
A stick was a sword, the bed a spaceship, the backyard a jungle.
Larping was the most natural thing in the world. Nobody had to teach us how to do it.
But somewhere along the way, we were disenchanted too.
Daydreaming suddenly became 'not paying attention', and following your dreams got replaced by 'be realistic' and 'get yourself a real job'.
This way the explorer of space ended up calculating spreadsheets, trapped inside four walls.
Not to mention that nowadays you also play a role: be it the good employee, the nice guy, or the quiet one.
In the beginning, you probably wrestled with it. But the longer you play a role, the harder it is for you to step out of it.
Ultimately, it gets automatic: past habits run future habits. Yesterday begins running tomorrow. Last week runs next month, and next month the coming years.
As William James put it: by thirty, the character has set like plaster. You've played the part for so long that you can't tell where the role ends and you begin.
"We are what we pretend to be, so we must be careful about what we pretend to be." Kurt Vonnegut.
And the point of all this isn't to make you hate your life even more. It's to make you see the script you've been handed, because realization is the first step to rewriting it.
So think about it. Did you consciously choose the role you play in this world, or was it assigned to you?
Do you live like an NPC someone wrote the script for, or the writer of your own myth and the main character of it at the same time?
Realizing this is one thing. Breaking the pattern is the other.
But the same way you once played your way into the role that is your identity now, the only way out is to play your way into the new one.
# Larping Is the Only Way Out
> "The things we have to learn before we can do them, we learn by doing them." Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics.
What makes an ordinary man become great, and common nations rise above others? This is the question historians have asked themselves for centuries.
Polybius, a Greek hostage who spent seventeen years in Rome, wrote in detail about one particular ritual, and pointed to it as a key reason why the Romans conquered most of the ancient world.
When a great man died, his family made a wax mask of his face and hung it in the hall of their house, next to the masks of all the great men before him. And at every great funeral, actors would put those masks on, dress in the ancestors' robes and ride through the Forum.
The young man would walk his house daily under the gaze of all these great men, and on rare occasions see them alive again, which, as Sallust wrote, set his soul on fire, and he grew up burning to equal and imitate his ancestors.
Generation by generation, this ritual built the most driven civilization in the history of humanity. Rome kept producing great scholars, generals and politicians.
The answer to why it worked was given two centuries earlier by Aristotle, who first said that man is the most imitative creature.
Everything we learn and do is in fact an act of imitation of something that has already been done before. The better we get at it, the more we begin adding our own things into it and make it ours.
Everything starts as a roleplay. You don't become someone first. You first play your part by imitating those who came before you, and with time and effort the part becomes you.
Because think about it, what is confidence?
Confidence is in fact playing confident, over and over again, until you no longer have to act.
What is competence?
It is the very same thing. As Aristotle said, we become builders by building.
The truth of the matter is, every single change we make in life starts as a larp.
Before you fall asleep, you have to first pretend to be asleep.
Before you become a great fighter, you have to first step into the ring like one and mimic the moves of the fighters you look up to.
And the ancients of Hermetic philosophy took this idea to its highest form.
The Corpus Hermeticum puts it directly: unless you make yourself equal to God, you cannot understand God.
Imagine yourself everywhere at once. On the earth, in the sea, in the heavens. Not yet born, young, old, dead, and beyond death.
Build your personal myth with a clear storyline from start to finish, and only then, you can larp your way into anything.
# Build Your Own Myth
> "Thus it is that I have now undertaken, in my eighty-third year, to tell my personal myth." Carl Jung, Memories, Dreams, Reflections.
Crafting your personal myth starts the same way as everything in this world was ever created: by utilizing your imagination.
Every building, every empire, every book existed first as an image in someone's mind. And so does the man you are about to become.
Out here, in the physical world, you are bound by your past, your circumstances and the opinions of others. The imagination of a man is the only place where none of it applies: in your mind you can be anyone, do anything and be anywhere.
So turn inward, rise above the time-space constraints, follow your deepest drives and most secret longings, and design the ideal version of you, the same way you would design your character in a video game.
To make it all easier, split it into different areas of your life, say body, mind and spirit.
Body: what does he eat? How often does he train? How does he dress, walk and carry himself in a room?
Mind: what are his core thinking patterns? What principles does he live by? What does he study, and what does he let permeate his head?
And spirit: what is he here to actually do? What drives him when no one is watching? What does he believe about himself that nobody could talk him out of?
And then, when you have it all before your eyes, written on a piece of paper, utilize your imagination once again to mythologize and exaggerate it even more.
According to Giordano Bruno, the Renaissance master of the art of memory, the mind only holds on to images that are striking, memorable and overly beautiful. The ordinary ones fade within a day.
That is why religious texts speak in miracles and parables. This is why modern brands sell a story and a feeling, not a product.
And similarly, every great mind who rose above his time had his own personal myth, one that was larger than life and impossible to forget.
Alexander the Great convinced his followers that he was the son of Zeus, conceived by a thunderbolt.
Julius Caesar told all of Rome his bloodline ran back to Venus.
Augustus let the story spread that his mother conceived him in the temple of Apollo.
I'm not trying to push you into megalomania and a god-complex straight ahead. What I'm trying to say is that you are free to be whoever you want.
You are the creator and the reality is your creation, shaped by whatever image you want.
# Decide You Are It Right Now
> "I am that I am." Exodus, chapter three.
Now that you have your myth written down and a sense of where you are going, we come to the essence of the whole larping thing.
Because a myth on paper is still just a story. It becomes yours the moment you decide to break the programming once and for all and step into it.
So what you need to do is gather all the willpower at your disposal to make one, big, definite decision that you already are this person.
Shift your paradigm once and for all. From thinking about it in future timelines: 'when I make it, when I have more time, when I'm ready', to 'I am now'. And trick your subconscious mind for good into operating from that potential.
Readiness never comes first. It is the result of the decision, not its condition.
Caesar once stood at the same edge, on the bank of the Rubicon. Crossing it with an army meant war with Rome itself and no coming back. He crossed it and exclaimed: 'the die is cast.'
So make your decision, and burn the ships behind you.
# Play the Character
> "Lifetime is a child at play, moving pieces in a game. Kingship belongs to the child." Heraclitus, fragment fifty-two.
And lastly: simply play the character.
Because a decision alone won't carry you. You can't overthink your way out of this.
Your conviction has to be followed by constant, relentless action that keeps proving to the old version of you that he is no longer in charge.
Every little act in character is proof for your subconscious mind that you are him.
Think about it like stone blocks. You add stone block to stone block, until eventually it turns into a mountain.
Begin by playing little parts of him. Then the bigger ones. Then yet bigger, until you play the whole man.
Mimic him in every detail. The clothes, the voice, the choice of words, the music, the scent.
Pretty much anything. It is your game, and you create your own character.
Naturally, you will sometimes wrestle with it. The old you is still somewhere in there, so you will slip and fall out of character. But then you have a choice: keep playing, or go back to the script someone else wrote for you.
Simply press 'play again'. And again. And keep proving to yourself that you are him.
And one day, you will wake up, look in the mirror and simply not recognize the man you once were.
The old role is gone, and all its limits with it.
